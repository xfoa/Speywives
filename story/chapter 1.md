# Chapter 1

_Cauld._ The kind that seeps into your bones, needlefelted in by sharp rain. The kind that requires heat from within to vanquish. _A helluva way to end a night_, thought Siobhan, taking another drag of her cigarette to both calm her nerves and warm her body. She peered over her arm in a futile attempt to survey the damage: her sleeve was ripped, but thankfully no visible blood.

Questions and blame and guilt and anger were filtering into her head as the curtain of adrenaline began to lift. _I shouldae seen this coming_-- no, she corrected herself: _I shouldae listened to it. I shouldae trusted myself._

Closing her eyes as she exhaled the acrid, comforting smoke, she shook her head as if these thoughts would lose their hold and fall off. _Stupid, stupid, stupid. Stupid._ She took another eager drag, hungry to make an already long night shorter.

There was nothing more tying her to this place tonight, and as the music continued to blare from the massive oak doors of the club, she thought she may as well take a taxi home. Stubbing out her half menthol on the wall and replacing what remained into its silver case, she dug out her phone and fumbled her route home in the app on autopilot.

_Naebody's reacted like_ that _tae bad news in a lang time,_ she thought. _Cannae shoot the messenger. Especially when you paid fur the message._ But the news had been particularly bad this time, and the client sketchy, and the Weird was screaming out a warning she had, in a millisecond, dismissed. It was hard not to blame herself in this moment, even though she knew that that's not where the blame should rightly be placed.

The taxi landed with a dull thud, and she reflexively tapped her phone on the door. With an inoffensive _ping_ of recognition, the door unlocked and she stepped inside, the gentle slam of the closing door cutting out the distant thumps and chatter as she sat.

With a low hum, the taxi took off. As the club and the North Bridge became insignificant under her, she let out noise, half sigh, half soft scream, releasing tension and anger now that there was no-one else around to hear her. Edinburgh streets zipped past unaware as she breathed deep, controlled breaths to try and steady herself.
